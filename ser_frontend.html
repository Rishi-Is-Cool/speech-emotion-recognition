How a run works

Three steps. Your audio is sent to the analysis server, processed in memory and discarded. Nothing is kept.

Give it audio

Record from your microphone or upload an audio file. Up to thirty seconds of natural speech; longer gives a steadier answer.

The model listens

Emotion2Vec+ turns the clip into a 768-dimension embedding; a locked linear classifier turns that into six probabilities.

You check its reasoning

The timeline splits the clip into five-second windows and shows which ones pushed toward the answer and which pushed against it. Click any window to hear it.

Read this before you trust a result

It is right about one time in three

Measured at roughly 33% on real voices across six emotions. Better than the 17% you would get guessing, nowhere near good enough to decide anything that matters about a person.

Confidence is not correctness

A 90% score means the classifier found the clip unambiguous, not that it is right. Read the confidence badge and the timeline together.

Long clips get approximate evidence

Anything past one window is split up, and the per-window numbers stop being the classifier's exact arithmetic. The app says so every time this happens.

It is not a diagnosis

No clinical, hiring, or safety use. This is a demonstration of a speech-emotion pipeline and its explainability, nothing more.

Tips for a cleaner recording
  • Pick a quiet room and switch off fans, air-con and the TV.
  • Keep the mic about 15–20 cm from your mouth.
  • A wired headset or earphone mic beats a laptop mic. Bluetooth mics lower the audio quality.
  • Speak naturally for as long as the recording allows; short pauses are fine.

Method

How the answer is produced

The whole pipeline, the numbers behind it, and an honest account of what it cannot do.

The pipeline

Conform the audio

Downmixed to mono and resampled to 16 kHz. No denoising, no voice-activity trimming, no normalisation — the model sees the recording as it was made.

Embed with Emotion2Vec+

The whole clip becomes one 768-dimension utterance embedding. The encoder is frozen; nothing about it was fine-tuned here.

Classify

A standard-scaled logistic regression maps that embedding to six probabilities. Deliberately linear — it is what makes the per-window attribution below meaningful.

Attribute along the timeline

The clip is cut into five-second windows, each embedded and scored on its own, and each window's push toward the winning class is reported.

Known limits

Per-window numbers are approximate on long clips

Attribution is exact only when a clip fits inside a single window. Past that, re-embedding each window separately does not perfectly reconstruct the whole-clip embedding — measured reconstruction error 2.18 on a real 44-second recording. Whenever that happens the result carries a warning saying so, and the right reading of the bars is supporting evidence, never exact arithmetic.

Trained on acted speech, used on real speech

Much of the training material is performed emotion. Real conversational speech is flatter and more ambiguous, which is most of the gap between benchmark numbers and the 33% measured here.

Six boxes for something that is not six boxes

Real speech carries blends, sarcasm, and no emotion at all. Forcing every clip into one of six labels throws that away by construction.
