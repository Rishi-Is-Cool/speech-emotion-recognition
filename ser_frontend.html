How a run works

Four steps. Your audio is sent to the analysis server, processed in memory and discarded. Nothing is kept.

Give it audio

Record from your microphone or upload an audio file. Up to thirty seconds of natural speech; longer gives a steadier answer.

The light model listens first

An MFCC model hears the whole clip in milliseconds. A router reads the opening seconds and the light model's confidence, and decides whether that answer can stand.

The heavy model steps in when needed

Emotion2Vec+, a 94-million-parameter speech encoder, analyses the clip only when the router escalates. Or pick Heavy model only to always use it.

You hear why

The timeline shows which five-second stretches pushed toward the answer and which pushed against it, and the page can read its reasoning out loud.

Read this before you trust a result

It is right about one time in three

Measured at 35% on 516 real recordings from four people, across six emotions. Better than the 17% you'd get by guessing, nowhere near good enough to decide anything that matters about a person.

Confidence is not correctness

A 90% score means the model found the clip unambiguous, not that it is right. Read the confidence badge and the timeline together.

Two models, one answer

Each recording is answered by either the light model or the heavy model, never both. The page always tells you which one answered.

It is not a diagnosis

No clinical, hiring, or safety use. This is a demonstration of a speech-emotion pipeline and its explainability, nothing more.

Tips for a cleaner recording
  • Pick a quiet room and switch off fans, air-con and the TV.
  • Keep the mic about 15–20 cm from your mouth.
  • A wired headset or earphone mic beats a laptop mic. Bluetooth mics lower the audio quality.
  • Speak naturally for as long as the recording allows; short pauses are fine.

Green Inference Engine

Spend the heavy model only where it pays

A light model listens to every recording. A router, trained on real voices, predicts whether the light answer would match the heavy model's. If not, the clip is worth ~24× more compute, and the heavy model answers instead. Watch it decide.

Pick a recording

Three acted clips from public datasets, with known labels. Acted speech is exaggerated and studio-clean, which is unlike the real voices this router learned from, so it sends all three to the heavy model. For a clip that stays light, try your own voice on Analyze.

Method

How the answer is produced

The whole pipeline, the two models behind it, the numbers, and an honest account of what it cannot do.

The pipeline

Prepare the audio

Downmixed to mono and resampled to 16 kHz. Nothing else: no denoising, trimming or normalisation, so the models hear the recording as it was made.

Light model

13 MFCCs plus their deltas and delta-deltas (coefficient 0 dropped), averaged over the whole clip into 72 numbers, then a small neural network (72 → 256 → 128 → 6).

Router (ASCA)

Twelve acoustic complexity features from the opening three seconds (SNR, energy, spectral shape, flux, entropy, MFCC statistics) plus the light model's own confidence: fifteen numbers into a logistic regression. It escalates when P(needs heavy) ≥ 0.63.

Heavy model

Emotion2Vec+ turns the clip into a 768-number embedding; a standard-scaled logistic regression turns that into six probabilities. It runs only on escalation, or always in Heavy-only mode.

Explain the answer

The clip is cut into five-second windows. For the heavy model each window's push toward the answer is linear arithmetic on its own embedding; for the light model each window is scored by the light model on its own. Pitch and loudness come from signal processing, not the model.

Known limits

Routing saves compute, not accuracy

On new people the light model answered 31% of clips, with accuracy 34.1% against 34.9% for the heavy model alone. The target was for the final answer to match the heavy model's on at least 90% of clips; on new people it matched on 75%. Four people is a small sample.

Trained mostly on acted speech

Much of the training material is performed emotion. Real conversation is flatter and more ambiguous, which is most of the gap between benchmark numbers and real-voice results.

Long clips get approximate evidence

A window is re-analysed on its own, without the context around it, so on clips longer than five seconds the per-window bars are supporting evidence rather than exact arithmetic.

Six boxes for something that is not six boxes

Real speech carries blends, sarcasm, and no emotion at all. Forcing every clip into one of six labels throws that away by construction.
